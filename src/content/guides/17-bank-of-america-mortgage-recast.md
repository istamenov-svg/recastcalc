## The short version

A **recast** lowers your monthly payment after you pay a lump sum toward your principal. Your interest rate and payoff date stay the same; your lender recalculates the payment on the smaller balance.

According to Bank of America's published mortgage FAQ, here's what it requires:

| Requirement | Bank of America's published terms |
|---|---|
| Fee | None: "There is no fee for a recast" |
| Minimum principal paid | $5,000, paid over the previous 6 months or within 30 days after your request |
| Payment history | At least your first 6 payments made, and the loan current |
| How often | Once every 12 months |
| Other conditions | The rules of whoever owns your loan (the investor) apply |

*Terms verified against Bank of America's published FAQ in October 2026. Policies change; confirm with Bank of America before sending a lump sum.*

## What makes Bank of America's terms notable

Two things stand out compared with the other servicers we track:

- **No fee.** Of the 13 servicers in [our servicer table](/recast/#lenders), Bank of America is the only one whose published terms say it charges nothing. The others that publish a fee list $150 to $700.
- **The $5,000 can be spread out.** Bank of America counts principal you've paid over the previous six months, so you don't necessarily need one large payment; several extra payments can add up.

## Who qualifies

Based on the published terms, you'll need:

- a Bank of America mortgage that's **current**, with at least the first six payments made
- **$5,000** or more in extra principal, paid in the six months before your request or within 30 days after it
- no recast in the past **12 months**
- a loan whose owner allows recasts. Bank of America notes that investor guidelines apply. Government-backed loans (Federal Housing Administration (FHA), Department of Veterans Affairs (VA), and U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) loans) are generally not eligible for recasting at the servicers that publish their rules; ask Bank of America about yours.

## How much could your payment drop?

Here's an example. The figures are illustrations, not a quote.

You owe $300,000 at 4.0% with 25 years left, paying about $1,584 a month. You pay $25,000 toward principal and request a recast:

- **New balance:** $275,000
- **New payment:** about $1,452 a month
- **Savings:** about $132 a month, with no fee, and you keep your 4.0% rate

Compare that with refinancing: when today's rates are higher than yours, refinancing would raise your rate, and [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/mortgages/learn/what-is-mortgage-recast) says refinance closing costs usually run 2% to 6% of the refinanced amount.

## How to request a recast

Bank of America doesn't publish a step-by-step process online, so confirm the details by phone or through your online account. A typical sequence:

1. **Confirm eligibility.** Ask whether your specific loan can be recast, given its owner and type.
2. **Make the principal payment.** Make sure it's applied to principal, not to future payments. Ask how Bank of America wants larger payments sent.
3. **Request the recast** within the time window, citing the principal you've paid.
4. **Watch for the new payment.** Ask when the new amount takes effect, and keep paying the current amount until then.

Questions worth asking:
- Can my loan be recast, given who owns it?
- Does the $5,000 have to be in one payment, or can it be several?
- How long does processing take, and when does my new payment start?
- Will anything else change, such as my escrow payment or my payoff date?

## Should you recast?

Recasting makes the most sense when you have cash that isn't needed elsewhere, your current rate is low, and you want a lower monthly payment rather than an earlier payoff. If you'd rather pay off the loan sooner, extra payments without a recast do that; see [should I recast my mortgage?](/guides/should-i-recast-my-mortgage/) for the full decision.
